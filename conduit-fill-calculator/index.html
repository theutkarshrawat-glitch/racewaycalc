How conduit fill is calculated

Add up the cross-sectional area of every conductor, then compare it with the allowed share of the raceway's inside area.

Total conductor area (Table 5 / Table 8) ≤ Raceway area (Table 4) × Fill limit (Table 1)

  1. Find each conductor's area in Chapter 9, Table 5 (insulated) or Table 8 (bare). Insulation matters: #12 THW is 0.0181 in², #12 THHN only 0.0133 in².
  2. Count every conductor, including the equipment ground (Note 3).
  3. Pick the fill limit from Table 1: 53% for 1 wire, 31% for 2, 40% for 3 or more (60% for short nipples).
  4. Compare the total with Table 4's area for the exact raceway type and trade size.

Worked example: 3 #10 THHN + 1 #10 bare in 1/2" EMT

3 × 0.0211 + 1 × 0.011 = 0.0743 in². 1/2" EMT is 0.304 in² inside, so fill is 0.0743 ÷ 0.304 = 24.4%, under the 40% limit (0.1216 in²). ✓

FAQ

What is the maximum conduit fill allowed by the NEC?

NEC Chapter 9, Table 1 limits fill to 53% of the raceway's inside area for one conductor, 31% for two conductors, and 40% for three or more. Nipples 24 inches or shorter may be filled to 60% (Chapter 9, Note 4).

How many #12 THHN wires fit in 3/4 inch EMT?

Sixteen. 3/4" EMT has 0.533 in² of inside area; 40% of that is 0.2132 in². A #12 THHN conductor is 0.0133 in², and 0.2132 ÷ 0.0133 = 16.03, which rounds down to 16. This matches NEC Annex C, Table C.1.

Does the ground wire count toward conduit fill?

Yes. Equipment grounding and bonding conductors count when calculating fill (Chapter 9, Note 3). Use Table 5 for insulated grounds and Table 8 for bare conductors.

When can I round up the number of conductors?

Only when every conductor in the raceway is the same size and insulation. Chapter 9, Note 7 lets you round up to the next whole number when the calculated decimal is 0.8 or more. For example, #8 THHN in 3/4" EMT works out to 5.83, so 6 are permitted.

Why does PVC hold fewer wires than EMT of the same trade size?

Trade size is a nominal name, not the inside diameter. 3/4" Schedule 40 PVC has 0.508 in² inside versus 0.533 in² for 3/4" EMT, and Schedule 80 has only 0.409 in². Always use the Table 4 row for the raceway you're actually installing.

What is jam ratio?

When pulling exactly three conductors, they can wedge side by side in a bend if the raceway inside diameter is 2.8 to 3.2 times the conductor outside diameter (Chapter 9, Table 1, Informational Note 2). The calculator flags that range so you can upsize the raceway or change the pull.

Does conduit fill tell me how many current-carrying conductors I can run?

No. Fill is only about physical space. With more than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway, Table 310.15(C)(1) adjustment factors reduce each conductor's ampacity, which often limits a circuit before fill does.
