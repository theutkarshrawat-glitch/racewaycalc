How voltage drop is calculated

Single-phase / DC: VD = 2 × I × Z × L ÷ 1000
Three-phase: VD = 1.732 × I × Z × L ÷ 1000
Table 9 AC: Z = R × PF + XL × sin(arccos PF)

I is load amps, L is the one-way length in feet, and Z is ohms per 1,000 ft of one conductor. The percent drop is VD ÷ source voltage × 100.

Worked example: 240 V, 44 A, 160 ft, #6 copper

Using the K-factor method: VD = 2 × 12.9 × 44 × 160 ÷ 26,240 = 6.9 V, which is 2.9% of 240 V. The load sees about 233.1 V. That's under the 3% recommendation. (Same answer as Mike Holt's published example.)

FAQ

What voltage drop does the NEC allow?

The NEC recommends (in Informational Notes to 210.19 and 215.2) no more than 3% on a branch circuit or feeder and 5% combined from the service to the farthest outlet. These notes are recommendations, not enforceable rules, unless your jurisdiction or a specific section (such as fire pumps or sensitive electronic equipment) requires a limit.

What formula does this voltage drop calculator use?

Single-phase and DC: VD = 2 × I × Z × L ÷ 1000. Three-phase: VD = 1.732 × I × Z × L ÷ 1000. I is the load current, L the one-way length in feet, and Z the conductor's ohms per 1,000 ft from NEC Chapter 9 Table 9 (AC), Table 8 (DC resistance), or the K-factor method (K = 12.9 copper, 21.2 aluminum).

Should I use one-way or round-trip length?

One-way. The 2 (single-phase) or 1.732 (three-phase) in the formula already accounts for the return path. Measure along the route the wire takes, including vertical runs.

What's the difference between Table 8 and Table 9?

Table 8 lists DC resistance. Table 9 lists AC resistance and reactance for conductors in PVC, aluminum or steel conduit, and lets you account for power factor. For #1/0 and smaller the results are close; for larger conductors, long feeders and motor loads, Table 9 is more accurate.

What current should I enter?

The actual load current at 100%. Don't use 125% for continuous loads or motors here; that factor is for sizing conductors for ampacity, not for voltage drop.

Does the recommended size meet ampacity too?

With "Also check ampacity" on, the recommended size must meet your voltage-drop target and have a 75°C ampacity (Table 310.16) at least equal to the load, and respects the 240.4(D) small-conductor breaker limits. You still need to apply temperature correction, adjustment factors and terminal ratings for your installation.

How do I fix too much voltage drop?

Upsize the conductor, run parallel sets (1/0 and larger), use a higher system voltage, shorten the run, or move the panel or transformer closer to the load.
